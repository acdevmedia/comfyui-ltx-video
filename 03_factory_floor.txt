INT. PRECISION ENGINEERING FACTORY – MORNING

A continuous documentary-style medium-wide shot tracks slowly sideways along the aisle of a working British engineering factory, with a concrete floor and rows of grey CNC machining centres. Bright, diffused morning daylight falls from high industrial windows and fills the aisle evenly, lighting faces clearly with soft shadows and natural skin tones, and catching clean highlights on the machined metal. Colours are neutral greys and blues with true whites. A machine operator in his fifties, in navy overalls and clear safety glasses, stands at a large enclosed machine holding a small machined steel part. He places the part into a fixture inside the machine, tightens the clamp with two quick turns, withdraws his hands and slides the transparent safety door shut with a click. He presses a green button on the control panel; behind the glass the spindle starts to turn and coolant begins to spray.

A female engineer in her forties, with tied-back dark hair, dark work clothes and safety glasses, walks up the aisle and stops beside him. She leans towards the glass and asks in a plain, matter-of-fact British English accent, "Is this the revised part?" The operator turns his head to her and answers in a steady, unhurried voice, "Yes. We changed the tolerance yesterday." He nods at the machine. "This is the first production run." She watches the spindle for a moment, nods once and says, "Good. Let me know when it's finished." She turns and walks away down the aisle.

The camera comes to rest in a steady medium-wide shot of the operator, who turns back and watches the spindle turning behind the glass as coolant runs down the inside of the door.

The audio is a working factory: constant ventilation hum, distant machinery, the click of the safety door and the rising whine of the spindle, with both voices clearly audible above it. No music.
