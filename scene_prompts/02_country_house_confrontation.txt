A continuous photorealistic wide shot in a grand English country-house drawing room in the afternoon: pale green walls, gilt-framed oil paintings including a portrait of a stern man, a lit fire in a carved marble fireplace on the left, warm table lamps, and soft daylight from tall windows on the right. A silver-haired woman in her sixties in a navy dress and pearl necklace stands by the fireplace with her hands clasped. Her brown-haired son in a pale blue shirt and dark trousers sits in a worn leather armchair by the window, legs crossed, fingers pressed to his temple, a glass of whisky on the side table beside him. The camera holds steady with a very slow push-in. In a clipped, icy received-pronunciation voice she says, "Your father knew about the missing money. Every penny." He drops his hand and sits up, his voice cracking: "Then why did he let me take the blame?" She smiles thinly. "Because I told him to, darling. You were always the most expendable." He stares at her. "I'm your only child." "Yes. I've been meaning to talk to you about that." He grips the chair. "Where is Father?" She glances down at the fire. "Warm." He looks at the fire, then back at her, his mouth falling open. She tuts. "Don't make that face. He always said he wanted to be cremated. I simply didn't wait." He grabs the whisky and drains it. "Mother, that's murder!" She adjusts her pearls. "It's a Grade Two listed fireplace, darling. Nobody is going to dig it up." A log shifts in the fire with a soft crunch. Hold on both of them as the old house creaks. No music.
