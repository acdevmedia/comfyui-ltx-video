A photorealistic science-fiction sequence in bright daylight high above an endless layer of white clouds under a deep blue sky. A wide aerial shot looks up at an enormous dark silver alien disc hanging motionless above the cloud tops, its curved hull covered in concentric ridged rings. Bright sun high on the left lights the top edge of the disc, while soft white light reflected up from the clouds fills its underside so the hull detail stays visible, and its vast shadow spreads across the clouds below. A row of massive segmented doors on its underside slides apart, opening a long rectangular hangar lit by rows of cold white lights, where a small matte-black stealth bomber sits on a launch platform: a wide, angular flying wing with no tail fins and two engines set into its upper surface. A hard cut transitions to a closer three-quarter front view of the black flying-wing bomber inside the hangar, lit evenly from above by the rows of cold white lights, which pick out the edges and panel lines of its matte-black surface; the low mechanical hum of the disc continues across the cut. The bomber's engines start with a deep rumble that climbs into a turbine roar, heat haze rippling behind the exhausts and small navigation lights blinking on along the wingtips. The launch platform rolls forward, carrying the bomber towards the open hangar mouth and the blue sky beyond. A hard cut transitions to a wide exterior shot from beside and below the disc, its giant curved underside filling the top of the frame in soft reflected cloud light, with the sun high on the left; the engine roar continues across the cut over rushing high-altitude wind. The small black bomber glides out of the hangar into open air and holds level, then lifts its nose, tucks its landing gear away and banks gently to the right, its flying-wing silhouette dark against the white clouds. It flies off towards the horizon with its engines fading, while the hangar doors slide shut behind it and the enormous disc hangs over the sky. The sound is the disc's low hum, heavy door machinery, the bomber's engines and the wind. No music.
