INT. POLICE INCIDENT ROOM – OVERCAST AFTERNOON

A continuous eye-level tracking shot moves slowly backwards through an untidy provincial police incident room as a weathered English detective in his late forties walks towards the camera. He has short greying hair, a lined face and a charcoal overcoat, and carries a brown case folder at his side. Soft overcast daylight from tall windows on his left falls across old wooden desks, metal filing cabinets and loose paperwork. The window light models his face clearly, with gentle shadows on his right side, natural skin tones and visible detail in the dark folds of his coat. Colours are muted greys and browns with neutral whites. The camera keeps him framed from the waist up.

He glances at a colleague standing just off-screen beside the camera, the corner of his mouth lifting into a small smile, and speaks in a measured, dry southern English accent, "The trouble with this case is that everybody remembers exactly what happened." He walks on in silence for a few paces, looks down at the folder, then lifts his eyes back to the colleague. "Unfortunately, none of them remembers the same thing." He stops walking. A beat of silence. The smile fades and his jaw sets. He speaks more quietly, "So perhaps we should work out which version is true..." He pauses. "...before we arrest anyone."

The camera slows to a stop in a steady medium shot of his face and shoulders, still lit softly from the window on his left. He holds the colleague's gaze without blinking, the folder still at his side, and the shot lingers on him in the quiet room.

The audio is close and natural: his voice is clear over soft footsteps, the faint hum of office lights and distant, indistinct voices from another room. No music.
