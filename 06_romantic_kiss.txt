A continuous photorealistic wide two-shot in an old English country-house drawing room at night: rain streaming down tall leaded windows, deep green walls hung with gilt-framed paintings, a log fire crackling in a stone fireplace on the left and warm table lamps casting golden light on both faces with natural skin and soft shadows. A slender dark-haired woman in her early thirties, hair pinned up, wearing a dark green satin slip dress, stands by the fireplace with her hands clasped, looking up at a tall brown-haired man with light stubble in a white shirt with rolled sleeves and dark trousers, one hand in his pocket. The camera holds steady with a very slow push-in. She smiles, her lip trembling, and says softly in an English accent, "I've rehearsed this conversation a hundred times." He tilts his head with a teasing smile and answers in a low English voice, "And how does it end?" She whispers, "Usually with you saying something catastrophically stupid." He draws a small velvet box from his trouser pocket. "I've brought my wife's engagement ring." Her breath catches. "You're married?" He shrugs. "Only technically. She's in the car." "Does she know about me?" "She's the one who sent me in. She wants the house." The woman stares at him for a long beat, glances at the expensive room around her, then shrugs, pulls him close by his shirt collar and kisses him slowly and tenderly. They part, foreheads touching. He whispers, "I love you." She smiles with her eyes closed: "Much better. Stick to that." Outside, a car horn honks twice, impatiently. Rain patters on the glass and the fire crackles. No music.
