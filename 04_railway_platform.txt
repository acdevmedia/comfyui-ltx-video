A continuous photorealistic wide shot on a wet Victorian railway platform in a northern English town at sunset: a red-brick station building with a large clock, an ornate iron canopy, glowing lamp posts, a platform sign marked 2 across the tracks, and a fiery orange and purple sky over hills and church spires, the warm lamp light and sunset glow reflected in the rain-soaked paving. A dark-haired woman in her early thirties stands on the platform in a dark green parka, tartan scarf and brown boots, a backpack on one shoulder, holding a train ticket, a battered brown wheeled suitcase beside her; she gazes off to the right, lips pressed tight. The camera holds steady with a very slow push-in. A breathless man's voice calls from off-screen in a northern English accent, "Emily! Don't get on that train!" She turns her head towards the voice, eyes blazing: "You had six years to say something, Tom!" His voice: "It's not about us! Why is your suitcase moving?" The suitcase rocks on its wheels. She doesn't look at it. "It's your mother." His voice cracks: "You put my mother in a suitcase?" She shrugs. "She called my lasagne brave, Tom. And she fitted. That's on her." A muffled old woman's voice shouts from inside the suitcase, "It was brave!" Without looking down, Emily nudges the suitcase with her boot. A long pause. His voice, quieter: "Is she coming to Edinburgh?" Emily smiles for the first time. "She's paying for the hotel. She just doesn't know yet." A station announcement echoes, a distant train rumbles and rain drips from the canopy. No music.
